Category	                       Risk Profile
#Malware/platform                     compromise Very low — signed firmware chain, controlled app distribution

#Telemetry	                          Lower than peers — this is documented, not marketing. Apple's differentiators that hold up under scrutiny: on-device processing for Siri suggestions/photos/typing
                                      (data never leaves the device), differential privacy aggregation, Intelligent Tracking Prevention actively breaking third-party trackers in Safari, Private Relay (iCloud+ feature) obscuring browsing from both carriers and Apple. Their business model is hardware sales, not ad inventory — Google and Meta monetize your data; Apple monetizes your devices

#Cross-site tracking	              Minimal — Apple is not an ad network. They accepted Google's billions to keep Google as default Safari search, which is a business arrangement, not a tracking integration

#Jurisdiction	                      US, CLOUD Act — same as the rest. This is where Apple's privacy posture weakens: much iCloud data (email, iCloud Drive photos in standard configuration, backups) is accessible to Apple and
                                      thus subpoenaable, because full end-to-end encryption only covers a subset (iMessage, HomeKit, HealthKit, passwords) by default. The Advanced Data Protection option
                                      extends E2EE broadly but must be opted in

#China concessions	                  Worth remembering given your provenance-based threat model: iCloud China-region data is operated by GCBD (state-linked entity),
                                      and Apple has complied with app-store takedown demands. Doesn't affect Canadian accounts, but speaks to corporate willingness to comply under pressure
#Shazam specifics	                  Audio fingerprints, location, and usage patterns are collected. Apple kept it operating as a free service post-acquisition — meaning the data flow continues
                                      to Cupertino instead of an independent Shazam Ltd.
